03 / iOS utility / Product design & engineering

UnitX
Making everyday conversion feel immediate

A unit converter designed around the way questions actually appear: type it, say it, scan it, save it, or pick up a familiar conversion from your wrist.

Role - Product design & SwiftUI engineeringFocus - Intent-first interaction, precision controls, multimodal inputStatus - Live on the App Store

Context

Back in 2025 I was in Japan, and my converter app stopped working the moment I lost signal.

I use one constantly, most of all when I travel - which is exactly where mine gave up. That was the push. I installed the first twenty converters the App Store offered me and audited them properly. They were mostly the same app: cluttered, years out of date, and unable to finish the one task they exist for without a banner ad in the way. Favourites sat behind a paywall. History and Apple Watch support were rare. Almost none of it worked offline. UnitX is the converter I wanted on that trip: an answer straight away - no network, no ad, no form to fill in first.

  • Where it started Twenty of the top-ranked converters, installed and used before any design work began
  • Audience People converting measurements or currencies in the middle of another task
  • Product challenge Give the answer immediately, offline and unblocked, while keeping exact manual conversion one tap away
  • Platforms iPhone with an Apple Watch companion for repeated utility moments

Design choices

Start with intent. Offer control when it helps.

I built the main surface to take short requests like “72 kg to lb” or “5 miles in km.” Manual controls sit beside it as a parallel route, not a buried fallback, and voice and camera entry take the typing out when the number is spoken or printed.

Type

Natural queries

Let people begin with the words they already have instead of a form to configure.

Choose

Manual precision

Keep categories, units, swapping, and values explicit for exact or repeatable work.

Capture

Voice and camera

Turn a spoken request or visible label into an editable starting point.

Competitive audit

The twenty apps wrote the brief.

Every row below is a reason I put one of them down, and the decision it produced in UnitX.

The five recurring findings across twenty converters, and what each one changed.
What the audit foundWhat UnitX does instead
The answer sat behind a banner adNo ads. The result is the first thing on screen, and nothing interrupts it.
Favourites were locked behind a paywallFavourites work on the free tier. Pro lifts the limit rather than unlocking the feature.
History was rare, or cleared itselfEvery conversion is kept and replayable, so a repeat request never starts from scratch.
Apple Watch support was rareA Watch companion carrying favourites and recents, for the moments the phone is not in hand.
Almost nothing worked without a signalUnit conversion runs entirely on device. Only live currency rates need the network.

Evidence

A single converter, with several useful ways in.

I kept one outcome - the answer - and let the way in change with the moment. The iPhone screen shows intent-led suggestions alongside the manual, microphone, and camera flows; the iPad view makes the same navigation model visible at a larger scale.

Outcome

Every rating it has had so far is five stars.

UnitX is live on the App Store, free, and holding a 5.0 average across 25+ ratings. The part that matters more than the number: people who never read this page keep naming the three things the audit set out to fix - that it works without a signal, that nothing is sold to them on the way to an answer, and that it stays out of the way.

Reflection

Flexibility works only when the default remains calm.

I kept the common request light, and left reliable ways out of it: manual input, an editable result, history, favourites, and Apple Watch follow-through. What I still want to test is how well recognition recovers when it misreads something, and which repeat conversions actually earn a place on the wrist.
