# Behance Posting Plan: UnitX And BuildX

## Recommendation

Post UnitX first, then BuildX.

UnitX has the broader consumer story and more input variety, which makes it a stronger opening Behance piece. BuildX should follow as a more technical, craft-heavy case study focused on precision, domain logic, and job-site utility.

## Updated Direction From The STUDIVA Reference

Use the STUDIVA case study as the presentation reference for pacing and visual language:

- soft editorial boards instead of article-like long copy
- oversized faded product wordmarks behind the hero
- rounded category pills near the top of boards
- one realistic or angled device composition per major visual section
- a calm three-column overview panel for context
- timeline and process boards before final UI
- pastel insight cards and comparison matrices
- generous whitespace, light shadows, and very short captions

The final posts should feel like designed vertical decks. They should not look like Markdown articles pasted into Behance.

## Series Positioning

Together, the two posts can position the portfolio around polished Apple-platform utilities:

- **UnitX:** smart, multi-input everyday conversion
- **BuildX:** professional, exact, domain-specific calculation

The series message:

> I design and ship utility apps where small workflows need to be fast, reliable, and polished.

## Behance Format

Use a scrollable editorial layout rather than a long raw article.

Each post should have:

- one strong cover
- 10-14 visual sections
- short section titles
- concise body copy
- annotated device screens
- one product map or flow diagram
- one design system section
- one reliability or QA section
- a closing reflection

## STUDIVA-Style Board Rhythm

Use this more specific rhythm for both posts:

1. **Cover Hero**
   Product name, giant ghosted wordmark, device mockup, pill tags, year.

2. **Intro Statement**
   One large paragraph that explains the product in plain language.

3. **Overview / Project Info / Services**
   Three-column panel with overview, platform/date/category, and role/services.

4. **Project Timeline**
   Four process columns with soft dividers and pill labels.

5. **Competitive Analysis**
   Pill-header matrix comparing alternatives against the product.

6. **User Jobs**
   Floating angled cards with real job moments.

7. **Problem / Opportunity**
   Short visual cards that show what makes the workflow painful.

8. **Product Architecture**
   Pill-based map from inputs or tools to outcomes.

9. **Core Flow**
   Large annotated device screen.

10. **Final Screens**
    4-6 phone mockups arranged as a polished screen gallery.

11. **Design System**
    Swatches, typography, buttons, cards, and key UI patterns.

12. **Trust / QA**
    Reliability, calculation logic, edge cases, or state handling.

13. **Reflection**
    Short closing statement with one strong takeaway.

## Section Templates

Use this rhythm for both posts:

1. **Hero**
   Product name, value proposition, device composition.

2. **Problem**
   One clear user pain, written in plain language.

3. **Opportunity**
   The design and product thesis.

4. **Product Map**
   A diagram of how the app is structured.

5. **Core Flow**
   The most important interaction.

6. **Secondary Flows**
   Supporting screens that prove depth.

7. **Design System**
   Color, typography, cards, controls, icon treatment.

8. **Trust**
   QA, calculation logic, reliability, edge cases, or monetization honesty.

9. **Outcome**
   What shipped and what improved.

10. **Reflection**
    One thoughtful closing insight.

## Writing Rules

- Avoid fake metrics unless there is real analytics evidence.
- Do not overclaim research if the project was independently designed.
- Keep captions short enough to sit beside visuals.
- Explain product decisions, not only screens.
- Mention SwiftUI and implementation only where it supports product credibility.
- Use concrete examples such as `500 g to lb`, `100 USD to INR`, `6/12 pitch`, or `4x12 drywall`.
